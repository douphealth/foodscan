# FoodScan 100.0.0: eight fixes to the 99.0.0 check-in, and four additions (label bridge, grade source, 7-day pulse, swap list)

Base: FoodScan 99.0.0 (this tree; 99.0.0 itself is still uncommitted on `release-99`, so this release sits on top of it). The brief was an audit of 99.0.0 with eight claimed defects and four proposed upgrades. Every claim was checked against the code first; all eight were real, one of them (the size of the cleanup) is smaller than claimed.

## The eight defects
| # | Claim | Found | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A new phone cannot reach "Restore backup" or "My Lens": the hub is removed when nothing is saved or logged | **Real** (`renderHub` removed the hub when both lists were empty, and the backup and lens controls lived inside it) | A compact welcome card (title, one line, a folded "My Lens and backup" block) is shown instead. It uses the same block as the full hub, so Restore backup and My Lens work from an empty profile. The hub still starts folded, so the scan button stays on the first screen. |
| 2 | A product link inside the compare dialog leaves the dialog covering the product page | **Real** (`sync()` reset the amounts row on a page change but never closed the dialog) | `sync()` closes the dialog whenever the page changes. Checked in the browser: tapping a product in the dialog opens the page with the dialog closed. |
| 3 | A megabyte-sized string is built on every page mutation | **Real** (the change key joined the raw `fs13:saved`, `fs13:history` and the other lists) | The key uses a signature of each list (length, first 48 and last 48 characters): the app writes new rows at the front and drops old ones at the back, so every add, delete and reorder changes it. The record map is also cached and rebuilt only when a signature changes (before, each call parsed up to a megabyte of JSON). |
| 4 | A 1.5 L bottle gets a one-tap "Whole pack (1500 ml)" | **Real** (`packOf` accepted up to 1,500) | A pack or unit becomes a one-tap amount only up to 600 g or ml (`PACK_ONE_TAP_MAX`). A typed amount still goes to 2,000. A multipack still offers one unit (`6 x 330 ml` -> 1 unit, 330 ml). |
| 5 | The compare table mixes 100 g and 100 ml and still crowns winners | **Real** | Every column says "per 100 g" or "per 100 ml"; when the pinned products are not all on one basis, a note says so and no column is marked best. |
| 6 | The data check misses a shared link and the label reader's numbers | **Real** (the card read only `fs13:saved` and `fs13:history`) | It also reads the app's own snapshot list (`fs591:source-snapshots`), looks again for five seconds (ten tries) while the app writes the history row, and lays the shopper's own label numbers (`fs85:label:<code>`) over the database record. See "Label bridge". |
| 7 | Energy in the compare table is not formatted like the hub | **Real** | `Math.round(kcal).toLocaleString('en' / 'el')`: 1,234 / 1.234. |
| 8 | 146 legacy chunks, about 2.86 MB, about 20 % of the bundle | **Real, smaller than claimed**: **135 files, 2.54 MB**, about 9 % of the assets folder, not 20 %. | See "Asset prune". |

## Additions
- **Label bridge.** Numbers the shopper typed or confirmed from a nutrition-table photo (`prices/reader-v85.js`, stored as `fs85:label:<barcode>`) now replace the database values field by field wherever the shopper gave a number, in the data check, the compare tray, and the check-in (200 g of a food whose label says 12 g sugars per 100 g logs 24 g). A label printed per serving is scaled with the serving size the shopper entered (no serving size: not used). The data check says so ("Numbers you entered from the label replace the database values"). The stored record is never changed; this is an overlay at read time. For a food with no database grade, the reader's exact label grade is used for the grade chip.
- **Where the grade comes from.** One line on the data check: *Published Nutri-Score*, *Calculated from the record*, *Estimated from similar products*, *Your label photo*, or *No grade on record*, each with a one-sentence tooltip. The mapping is the app's own grade states (`reported` = published, even when FoodScan reproduced it point for point; `estimated` = calculated from the record; the grade card's `similar` estimate; a saved label). The brief's "Official Open Food Facts" is called "Published Nutri-Score" because Open Food Facts is a community database, not an official one.
- **Last 7 days** on Home: one bar per day (sugars against the WHO 50 g), days logged, average sugars and salt over the days that have amounts, days over a limit, and the biggest swap win on your list. A day that has only 98.1 check-ins (no amount) counts as logged but is not averaged. The card says a food that does not report a nutrient adds nothing, so averages can be low. A heavy day is a tall bar, never a failure message.
- **Swap list ("Swap and save").** Every healthier-swap row on a product page has an "+ Swap list" button. The list on Home shows each swap with the product it replaces and its grade, and the lowest current recorded price with store and unit price (`GET /api/prices/peek`, the same daily data the swap rows use; a price older than 7 days is marked older and never added up; nothing is estimated). The summary counts swaps, grade steps gained, the total at current prices (one currency only), and how many are cheaper or dearer per kg or litre than the product you scanned (same basis, both current). Stored in this browser (`foodscan:list:v100`, up to 30), covered by backup and restore.

## Asset prune
`assets/` had 135 files from builds 7540 to 8300 that no page, module, worker, service-worker list, test or tool names (names were searched in every text file of the repository, transitively from each page). They are removed: **135 files, 2,542,299 bytes**, about 9 % of the assets folder (29.4 MB to 26.8 MB; the rest is data). 13 files from those builds stay because a page still loads them or a test or these notes name them (`app-V52SOTA1-v7540.css`, five `-v7540.mjs` helpers, three `-v7540.js` chunks, `search-index-v7540.json`, the three `-v8100.mjs` engine sources). A test (`qa/daily100.test.mjs`) walks every static import from the four pages and fails on a missing file. In the browser, the requests I inspected on a full load of Home and of a product page (about 280 of 480) had no 404. Names built at run time from pieces would not be found by a text search; the app bundle spells its chunk names out, and nothing loaded was missing.
One old guard changed: `qa/core.test.mjs` required at least 33 `-v8300.js` files to exist; it now requires none, and keeps the twin check for any that return.

## What was not built
Nothing from the earlier long roadmap changed (verification tiers, source-priority merge, OFF dump, IndexedDB catalogue, retailer feeds, sparkline, shelf-tag and receipt scanning, Smart Basket, a simpler Home): see `RELEASE-99.0.0.md` for each reason. The "12.4 % ungraded" figure in the brief was not re-measured here.

## Checks
- `qa/daily100.test.mjs` (21 tests): pack cap (1.5 L, 1 L, 601 g, 600 g, 500 ml, 6 x 330 ml), label key to GTIN-14, label per 100 g and per serving (30 g serving: x 3.333), a drink label, malformed labels, `mergeLabel` (label wins where given, database keeps the rest, record untouched), check-in from a merged record (200 g x 12 g / 100 = 24 g), grade source in all five states, `weekPulse` (window, legacy day, averages 36.7 g and 0.13 g by hand, month and leap-day edges), swap list (UPC-A and EAN-13 are one row, 30 max, junk ignored), grade steps (E to A is 4), list summary (3.20 total, older price excluded, mixed currency gives no total, per-kg only on the same basis), the hub-file fixes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, both languages carry every new string, and the static import walk.
- Browser (phone width, Greek and English): welcome card on an empty profile (Restore backup and five lens chips present, the line visible); week bars match hand arithmetic (Nov 22: cola 35.0 g + bar 40.0 g = 75 g, 150 % of the limit, average of four days 32.05 shown as 32.1); swap buttons on Nutella's five swaps and the list rows; the swap-list price line with real daily data from the extracted zip (Coca-Cola Zero 5449000214911: €0.91 at Bazaar, €2.76/l; no cheaper/dearer claim because the other product has no matching current unit price) and with a stubbed response for the comparison (€4.00/kg against €6.00/kg: "cheaper per kg or litre: 1"); a direct load of a product with the history cleared and a saved label (salt 0.5 g and 538 kcal from the label shown, the label note shown, source "Published Nutri-Score"); the compare dialog with a food and a drink (two basis labels, note, no winner, 1,234 kcal) and its closing after a product link.
- `node build.mjs --check`: the 23 modules rebuild `_worker.js` exactly (version string only). Full suite `node --test qa/*.test.mjs`: **198 tests, all pass** (177 before this release plus 21 new). Three existing tests changed: `qa/core.test.mjs` and `qa/daily98.test.mjs` pin the new version, and `qa/core.test.mjs` no longer requires the pruned v8300 generation to exist. `src/worker/manifest.json` records `core.js` at 35,957 bytes (the version string is one character longer).

## Limits
- Tested in a desktop browser at phone width, not on a phone camera or a real phone. The cheaper-per-kg comparison in the swap list was checked with a stubbed price response (no two real products with matching current unit prices were at hand); the price line itself was checked with real data.
- Once, on a first load of a product page, a price lookup from the swap rows (`prices/list-prices-v90.js`, 90.0.0 code, not changed here) got a 400 from `/api/prices/peek` (the endpoint takes 1 to 5 valid barcodes) and the next request worked. Not reproduced, not diagnosed. The swap list's own lookups ask for at most five codes with a valid check digit, and a failed lookup shows nothing rather than "no recorded price".
- The label bridge needs the shopper to have confirmed numbers in the label reader; no number is read behind their back. The overlay is per browser.
- Reference values are population limits, not advice for one person. The WHO sugar limit is for free sugars; the label gives total sugars, so the figure over-counts for milk, fruit and plain yoghurt.
